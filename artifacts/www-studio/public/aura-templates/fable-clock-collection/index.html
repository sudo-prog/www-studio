Twelve ways to tell the same moment

Every clock below is alive and reading your computer's time right now. Same seconds, twelve dialects: Swiss precision, glowing gas, folded cards, sand, plain English.

—
i

Railway

Hans Hilfiker, 1944. The seconds hand laps the dial in 58.5 seconds, then waits at twelve for the minute impulse — national punctuality, faithfully reproduced.

ii

Word grid

One hundred and ten letters; the right ones wake up. Rounded to the nearest five minutes, the way people actually speak.

iii

Binary LEDs

Binary-coded decimal: each column is a digit, rows are 8·4·2·1 from top to bottom. Read the lit dots downward.

iv

Daylight arc

The sun travels the arc from a fixed sunrise to sunset; at night it rests below the horizon and the arc waits for morning.

v

Nixie tubes

Neon-filled glass, cathodes stacked ten deep. The orange glow is the gas discharge; the faint mesh is the anode grid.

vi

Concentric rings

Outer ring sweeps once a minute, middle once an hour, inner once every twelve. Three orbits, one center.

vii

Flip cards

Split-flap mechanics: the upper leaf falls, the lower leaf catches. A hinge line runs through every digit.

viii

Polar dots

Sixty seconds on the rim, sixty minutes inside, twelve hours at the core. Dots fill clockwise and empty at the top of each cycle.

ix

Plain English

—

Told the way a grandparent would: quarter past, twenty to, nearly noon. Precision is overrated at the dinner table.

x

Twenty-four hours

One revolution per day, midnight at the bottom, noon at the top. The shaded half is night; the hand shows where in it you stand.

Seven-segment

Casio-grey liquid crystal, 1983. Unlit segments stay faintly visible, as they always did in a real LCD.

All twelve read the same clock; only the storytelling differs.
