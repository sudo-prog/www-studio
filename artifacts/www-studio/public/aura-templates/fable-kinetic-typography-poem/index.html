I / IX
Scroll
I

The sea does not sleep;

it only lowers its voice,

turning the long dark over like a stone

it has carried since morning.

II

Salt in the air, salt on the rail,

the moon laying down a road

no boat has ever managed to follow.

III

Listen — the hush

between waves

is the widest thing you will ever hear.

IV

Under the black glass the fish carry

lanterns they do not know they carry,

and the deep goes on being deep.

V

Tide fills the tide pools one by one

the way sleep fills a house:

first the low rooms, then the rest.

VI

Somewhere a bell buoy leans, and rights itself,

and leans — a slow iron heart

keeping time for no one.

VII

The stars fall in and are not drowned.

They float there, cold and patient,

learning the grammar of the swell.

VIII

What the sea knows it says in one word,

over and over, in the dark,

and the word is almost your name.

IX

Stay until the grey comes up in the east.

The night will fold itself away

like a net drawn in, dripping light.
